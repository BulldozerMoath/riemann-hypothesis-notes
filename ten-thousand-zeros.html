Why "checked up to height T" is a proof, not a suggestion

The method Stage 6 makes possible.

Every known "RH verified up to height T" headline rests on one trick, and it's worth seeing it work directly. Take the first 200 zeros located by searching only along the critical line (using Hardy's real-valued Z(t) from Stage 7) — the last one sits at height T = 396.382. Separately, run Stage 6's argument-principle count N(T), which knows nothing about where on the strip the zeros are, only how many there are in total:

The cross-check

Zeros found on the line up to T = 396.382: 200. N(396.382), counted independently by contour integration: 200. They match exactly — so there is no room left over the microscope hasn't looked in. That's what makes numerical verification of RH genuinely rigorous for the range it covers, not just suggestive: it's the argument principle plus a direct search, agreeing on a headcount.

The dip near zero is the point: independent (Poisson) points would happily sit right on top of each other, but the zeros visibly repel — small gaps are suppressed, exactly the GUE signature Montgomery and Dyson noticed over tea in 1972.

The explicit formula, converging in front of you

ψ(100) computed directly from primes is 94.0453. Stage 8's formula rebuilds it from zeros alone: the main term x, minus Σxρ/ρ, minus log(2π), minus the trivial-zero sum — adding one conjugate zero-pair at a time.

running approximation to ψ(100) as zero-pairs 1–100 are added
running approximation true ψ(100) = 94.045

It doesn't creep in monotonically — it can't, the sum is only conditionally convergent (Stage 8.4's honest caveat) — but the oscillation visibly tightens around the true value as more zeros are included, with no free parameters anywhere. Every one of those hundred numbers is an actual zero of ζ, doing actual work.
